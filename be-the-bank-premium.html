What is this, really?

A tiny game that lets you feel how a new kind of bank works — by running one yourself for two minutes.

1 What am I playing?
You're pretending to be a bank. You build little branches, and each branch makes you money every second — like a lemonade stand that keeps selling on its own. The more branches you have, the more you earn. It's a "watch your money grow" game, the kind people call an idle or tycoon game.
2 What's the catch?
To build a branch, you spend money — and that money is gone for good (it gets "burned"). That's on purpose. And halfway through, the game throws a panic at you: pretend customers all rush to pull their money out at once. This is called a bank run, and it's the scary thing that can break real banks.
3 So what's the big choice?
When the panic hits, you can grab your money and run with everyone else — but leaving costs a fee that gets huge when everybody leaves at the same time. Or you can stay calm and hold. If you hold, you actually get paid from the fees that all the panickers are paying. So the surprise lesson is: the person who panics loses, and the person who stays patient wins.
4 What kind of simulation is it?
It's a behavior simulation — it's not showing you exact prices or real numbers. It's showing you the incentives: what the smart move is, and how the system nudges everyone toward it. Think of it like a driving-school simulator: not a real car, but real enough to teach you the rules of the road.
How this ties to the actual project

STANDARD is a real crypto project — a "central bank" that runs entirely on code, with no people in charge. The whitepaper describes exactly the machine you just played: you hold a charter (your bank), you open branches that earn the coin, building them burns the coin, and when people try to cash out all at once, a rising exit fee punishes the panic and rewards whoever stays.

The whole point of STANDARD is that it defends itself: when money flees, the code automatically makes leaving expensive and makes staying pay. This game is the fastest way to get that idea without reading a single page of the whitepaper.
